# Forensic baseline — 2026-09-05

Full disk analysis of the machine, captured while resolving a chronic near-full condition
(3.85 GB free → 24 GiB free). Use these numbers as the comparison point; a block that has
grown far past its figure here is the thing to investigate.

**Machine:** MacBook, Apple Silicon, macOS 26.6.1 (25G76), 245 GB internal SSD, locale `pl_PL`,
Siri language `en-US`, Apple Intelligence **not** available.

## Volume layout (the truth `df -h /` hides)

| Volume | Consumed | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Data (`disk3s5`) | 196 GB | Everything you and your apps write |
| Preboot (`disk3s2`) | **18 GB** | Boot files + cryptexes. **Normal** on Apple Silicon (~12.4 GB baseline; Intel's 1–2 GB figure does not apply). Untouchable. |
| System, sealed (`disk3s1s1`) | 16 GB | macOS itself, read-only |
| Recovery (`disk3s3`) | 2.6 GB | Recovery OS |
| VM (`disk3s6`) | ~0 | Swap lives in `/private/var/vm` (2 GB sleepimage) |

`df -h /` reports only the 16 GB sealed volume and is useless for triage.

## The 2026-09-05 incident — Gemini telemetry leak

The machine was not full of accumulated junk. `~/Library/Caches/CCTClearcutLogger` was growing at
**336 MB / 30 s (~39 GB/hour)** and had rebuilt to 10.9 GB within 35 minutes of a complete wipe.

Spool contents, from `strings`: ~375 identical records per 500 KB (~1.3 KB each, ≈8,600/sec):

```
Could not retrieve Pseudonymous ID. Error domain: %@ code: %@
-[GIPPseudonymousIDStore pseudonymousIDWithoutCheckingEnabledStatus]
googlemac/iPhone/Shared/Metrics/PseudonymousID/Classes/GIPPseudonymousIDStore.m
  → NSCocoaErrorDomain, each with a 15-frame stack trace

Could not close log for log source %@, error domain: %@ error code: %@
-[CCTLogWriter flushInternal:error:]
googlemac/Shared/Metrics/Clearcut/Classes/Private/CCTLogWriter.m
  → NSPOSIXErrorDomain
```

Telemetry fails → the failure is reported *as telemetry* → that write fails → repeat. Upstream
GoogleDataTransport has had a disk cap since v7.4.0 (`GDTCORDirectorySizeTracker`), so this is a
defect, not design. Gemini.app 1.107.3.828.

It did **not** reproduce on a subsequent launch–use–quit cycle: intermittent. Absence of growth
is not proof it is fixed.

## What was reclaimed, and how durable

| Action | Freed | Durable |
|---|---|---|
| Gemini spool (after quitting the app) | 11 GB | Until it recurs |
| iOS 26.2 runtime deleted + its dyld cache self-cleaned | 11.6 GB | **Yes** |
| `~/Library/Caches` sweep | 6 GB | No |
| Antigravity ×2 installs, Unity leftovers (app long gone), Pixelmator container | 3 GB | **Yes** |
| `brew cleanup` | 0.6 GB | No |
| 11 orphaned iOS 26.2 sim devices | ~2 GB | Yes |

End state: **24 GiB free**, simulator runtimes 19.3 GB → 11.5 GB (iOS 26.5 + watchOS 26.2 kept).

## Traps hit during this session

- Deleting the runtime appeared to fail and free space *dropped* 8 GB — CoreSimulator was
  transiently rebuilding dyld caches mid-delete. `simctl runtime delete` is **asynchronous**;
  wait and re-check before concluding it failed.
- Nearly `rm -rf`'d a stale `~/Library/CloudStorage/OneDrive-Osobisty` holding 2019–2022
  conference decks as **cloud placeholders with no local copy**. Deleting a live sync root
  propagates to the cloud. Space gain would have been 1.3 MB.
- Recommended disabling Apple Intelligence for "9–12 GB" based on a research agent's inference
  from the presence of `UAF_Siri_*` assets. Wrong — AI is blocked on `pl_PL` and was never on.
  One `defaults read com.apple.CloudSubscriptionFeatures.optIn` would have caught it.
- An app "last used" scan showed `(null)` for Xcode, which is in daily use. Metadata is unreliable.
